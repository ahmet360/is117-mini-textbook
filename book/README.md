# One White T-Shirt, Many Meanings

A first-year guide to persuasion, brand archetypes, visual design, and thoughtful AI-assisted work.

How can the same plain white T-shirt seem adventurous, deliberate, rebellious, or welcoming? This mini textbook follows one imaginary product to show how communication and design shape interpretation. The final chapter applies those ideas to a repeatable workflow with specifications, checks, Git, and human judgment.

## Read the Chapters in Order

1. [Persuasion and Human Decision-Making](01-persuasion.md) — Attention, trust, influence principles, framing, and ethical choice.
2. [Brand Archetypes and Meaning](02-archetypes.md) — Twelve narrative patterns and the identities a product can invite.
3. [Modernism, Postmodernism, and Visual Language](03-design-language.md) — Design history, visual tensions, and museum research.
4. [One White T-Shirt, Four Different Invitations](04-white-tshirt-case-study.md) — Four complete concepts using the same physical shirt.
5. [From Human Intent to a Versioned Result](05-synthesis.md) — Directing AI, checking its work, and taking responsibility for the outcome.

## How to Use This Guide

Read Chapters 1–3 for the individual lenses, compare the four concepts in Chapter 4, and use Chapter 5 to guide your own work. Each chapter includes a Mermaid diagram and ends with a short reminder. Follow the previous, next, and guide links to move between chapters.

View the Markdown on GitHub to see the diagrams rendered. In an editor that does not support Mermaid, the fenced text is the diagram's source; the surrounding prose and tables explain the same ideas.

All T-shirt campaigns, headlines, and product stories are fictional classroom examples. Links beside factual explanations lead to source material. Sections labeled **External research suggestions** identify investigations you can carry out yourself; they do not substitute for verified collection records.

After reading, choose one concept and revise it for an audience you understand. Keep the physical product fixed, explain your choices, and separate evidence from interpretation. Before submitting, inspect the sources and make at least one editorial improvement you can explain in class.

For the practical's issue, branch, commit, and submission process, return to the [project instructions](../README.md). The [specification](../SPEC.md) lists the required artifacts.
